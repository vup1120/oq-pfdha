# Figure 4 — Caption

## English caption

**Figure 4.** Distributed (secondary) fault displacement versus distance
from the principal fault trace *r*, organized by faulting style:
(a) normal, (b) reverse, and (c) strike-slip. For dip-slip panels (a, b),
*r* is signed (negative = footwall, positive = hanging wall) and curves
are drawn continuously across *r* = 0; the strike-slip panel (c) is shown
for *r* ≥ 0 only. Magnitude-dependent models are evaluated at *m* = 6.5,
and the Visini regression uses a default dip of 60° for both normal and
reverse panels. The percentile of the plotted curve differs by model and
is labeled explicitly in the legend: Youngs et al. (2003) is shown at the
85th and 95th percentiles of *D/MD* per the source publication; Visini
et al. (2025) is the median (50th percentile) of the lognormal ln(*Y*)
regression for each combination A, B, and C; Moss et al. (2022) is the
50th-percentile *d/MD* envelope (Table 5.7 of GIRS-2022-05) under both
its gamma and envelope modes; Petersen et al. (2011) is the median
(50th percentile) recovered from the lognormal exceedance regression of
Eq. 18. Each curve is plotted only within its published applicable
magnitude and distance range (Valentini et al., 2025, Table 4). Pixel
size does not enter the Visini median displacement regression (it
modulates only the conditional rupture probability shown in Figure 3),
so a single curve per combination is drawn; likewise, the Petersen
Eq. 18 regression contains no cell-size term, so panel (c) shows a single
curve. The Visini curves spike at *r* ≈ 0 because the model's TPFm
smoothing window collapses to a point at the principal trace; this is
the documented model behavior, not a numerical artifact. Moss et al.
(2022) is included in panel (b) although it lies outside the Valentini
Figure 16 inventory.
